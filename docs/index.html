The page gets the whole window.

Backlight is a small web browser for Mac. Tabs wait at the left edge and the address at the top, and neither takes room until you reach for it. It is written in Swift on the WebKit that ships with macOS, and your AI agent can use it too.

Small, but it does a lot.

All of it is in the app today. Search, or pick a part.

Windows and edges

The page takes the whole window

Tabs sit in a sidebar and the address in a top bar. Both slide in when the pointer reaches their edge, and either can be pinned open.

Sidebar only

Settings can drop the top bar. The sidebar then holds back, forward, the site and its buttons. A new install starts this way.

Full screen

On a Mac with a notch the top bar runs around it. The menu bar and the Dock stay hidden until you press against the edge.

A new tab is a field

It opens over the page you are on, and the tab exists once you enter something. It suggests tabs, bookmarks and history, and matches letters in order (“gthub”) or two initials (“gh bl”).

Keywords and commands

Type a site's keyword and a space (“yt lofi”) to search it. Start with > to run any menu command.

Tabs, groups and profiles

Pinned tabs

Three to a row, as tiles above the rest.

Tab groups

A coloured name that folds, with a line of that colour down its tabs. Right-click a tab to add it.

Tabs stay under their parent

A tab opened from a link sits under its source, and closing it takes you back there.

Profiles

Coloured dots above the pinned tabs. Each profile has its own cookies, sign-ins, tabs, history and bookmarks. Extensions, zoom and site permissions are shared.

Split view

Right-click a tab or link and choose Open Side by Side. Drag the divider; the framed half has the keyboard.

Private tabs

They share one cookie jar kept in memory, which goes when the app quits. They are never saved and never shown to an agent.

Quiet tabs

A tab off screen is frozen, and unloaded after six hours or when memory runs short. A tab with unsent typing, sound or a call is left alone.

Reading and looking

Reader ⌥⌘R

Just the article, in light, sepia or dark, serif or sans, in five sizes.

Dark pages ⇧⌘D

A page that stays white while macOS is dark gets dark colours, pictures untouched. Off until you turn it on.

Peek ⇧ click

A link's page in a panel over the one you are reading; Esc puts it away. Hold ⌘ over a link for a smaller preview.

Translate Page

The system's own translation, done on this Mac.

Zoom, by site ⌘+ ⌘−

Set once and remembered for the site, except in private tabs.

Picture in picture ⇧⌘P

Just the video, in a window you can resize. A playing video goes there by itself when you leave its tab.

The page as one picture ⇧⌘R

Copies the whole page, top to bottom, as a single image.

Pick Color from Screen

In the View menu: macOS's eyedropper, with the color's code copied to the clipboard.

Saving

A download button for photos and videos

On the sites you list, a small button appears over a large photo or video. The list starts empty.

Videos sent in pieces

A video the page loads in pieces is joined into one mp4: an HLS playlist (AES-128 included), or a picture file and a sound file.

Downloads you can steer

Pause, resume, cancel and restart, with speed and time left. Paused ones and the last hundred finished survive quitting.

Copy address without the tracking ⇧⌘C

Removes utm_, fbclid, gclid and share codes, but never a video's t or a search's q. ⌥⇧⌘C copies the address as it is.

Import

Bookmarks and history from Chrome and its kin, Safari and Firefox, and a password file (CSV) from Chrome and its kin.

Privacy and the network

No telemetry, no account

Only the pages you open, plus a daily check for a newer version that Settings turns off.

Ads and trackers

Blocked by uBlock Origin Lite, the Safari extension from the App Store, YouTube's ads included.

Tracking parameters

Links you follow lose fbclid, gclid and the like, using the list macOS keeps for Safari.

What a site may do

The lock shows how the page was fetched, what it may do (location, notifications), what it stores here and its zoom.

Google keeps your region

Searches carry the Mac's language and region, so a VPN doesn't switch the results.

Extensions

Chrome Web Store, folders, Safari

Install from the Chrome Web Store (signature checked), an unpacked folder, or a Safari extension inside its app. One set serves every profile.

Talks to Mac apps

Bitwarden's Touch ID through its desktop app works.

For developers

Develop and Debug menus

As in Safari: Web Inspector, console, page source, timelines and WebKit's experimental features.

Settings kept per site

A site's user agent, color scheme and switches (JavaScript, images, styles) are saved and applied when you visit it.

Any user agent, any size

iPhone, iPad, Chrome, Firefox or your own, at phone, tablet or typed-in sizes.

Pick Element for the Agent

Point at an element and the agent gets its selector, text, HTML, styles, framework components and the page's recent errors.

Quiet by build.

It sends nothing but the pages you open and, once a day, a check for a newer version. Settings turns that off.

  • No telemetryNo analytics, crash reports or account.
  • No dependenciesSwift and the frameworks that ship with the Mac.
  • Updates by handChecked against GitHub's SHA-256 and the app's signature, and never installed without your say.
  • The sourceMIT licence, on GitHub. Releases are built from a clean clone.

What it cannot do yet

  • Passkeys.WebKit gives them to a browser only with an entitlement Apple grants on request, tied to a paid developer account.
  • Chrome's own tools.An agent here can't emulate touch or throttle the network.
  • Pages built only for Chrome.It is WebKit, like Safari, so a few of those misbehave.

Questions.

Why WebKit and not Chromium?

WebKit already ships with every Mac and macOS keeps it up to date, so the browser can be a few megabytes of Swift. A few pages built only for Chrome will misbehave, as they do in Safari.

Does it run Chrome extensions?

Many. WebKit's extension engine loads them from the Chrome Web Store, a folder or a Safari app, and one set serves every profile. An extension that needs Chrome's debugger won't work.

Does it support passkeys?

Not yet. Apple grants browsers that entitlement on request, tied to a paid Developer ID. Password managers that work as extensions, such as Bitwarden, do work.

What does it send, and to whom?

Only the pages you open and a daily check for a newer version, which Settings turns off. What an agent reads, when you let it, goes to that agent.

Why does the download button start with an empty list?

A button over every picture on every site gets in the way. You add the sites you want it on, in Settings.

Why Backlight?

A backlight is the light behind a screen. The page is lit, and the tabs and address stay out of the way until you reach for them. It was called BasicShell until 0.9.0.
